About Stefanie

She welcomed you in.
Then she kept you.

Stef had a way of making people feel that they belonged. You didn't stay a guest for long. You became part of her family, and somehow it felt like you had always been there.

She was honest. She saw our quirks, made fun of them, loved us for them, and recognized that they were often the very same quirks she carried herself.

And she paid attention. She would leave a book on your desk because she thought you would love it. She would bring you a drink or some food because she knew exactly what would make you happy.

“You became part of her family.
You were a lifetime family member.”

The Stefanie effect

Come for the visit.
Leave as family.

She didn't just welcome people. She made belonging feel inevitable.

There was always another chair, another story, another drink poured, another plate passed across the table. Her hospitality wasn't formal. It was personal.

She loved to talk about food

Food was a conversation,
a curiosity, a joy.

She loved talking about what you were eating, what you had discovered, what you should try next, and what sounded especially good that day.

She didn't just enjoy food. She thought about it. She wanted to try it. She wanted to hear what you thought about it.

“I love me some good soup.”

♨

A very Stefanie kind of thought

Good soup.

And probably a conversation about what we should eat next.

A little piece of Poland

Food was one of her ways of saying, “You're family.”

There are the things that taste like Stef: pierogi shared around a table, and those special cookies that somehow made their way into people's hands. Food carried memory, tradition, generosity, and a very clear message that you were welcome here.

Some traditions are recipes.
Some are the people who make you feel at home.
